What a "Semrush" does and where each piece of data comes from

An SEO suite pulls together, on one screen, data from very different sources: your own site, official Google and Bing dashboards, and data the company buys or scrapes. You get the first two for free. Knowing where each number comes from is what separates what you can build yourself from what only exists as a paid product.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The layers of an SEO suite and where each one comes from
  2. 2What your own site's data answers for free
  3. 3What only exists as paid: exact volume, competitors, full backlinks

Goal: separate, function by function, what your site gives you for free from what only exists with paid data, and install the 5 open tools.

01 · The layers of a "Semrush"

From top to bottom: from data that is only yours to data that someone sells. Tap each layer.

Estimate from the 10/19/2026 research, not measured: the free core covers most of the day-to-day needs of someone who looks after their own site; it does not cover market intelligence on third parties.

The first four layers belong to your site and come free. The last one is market data: someone collected it and charges for it.

02 · Function by function: where the data comes from

The functions suites sell, and the open substitute for each one.

1. Keyword

Paid tools mix clickstream, SERP scraping, and the Keyword Planner. For free you get your site's real queries in Search Console, autocomplete, and Google Trends.

Trends is a relative index, not a count of searches. Exact volume does not exist for free.

Honest limitSearch Console shows only the searches your site has already appeared in, and it hides rare queries (the "anonymized" ones).

2. Position

Search Console gives your site's real average position for each query. Tracking competitors' positions requires scraping Google, which violates the terms and gets blocked.

For your own site, free is enough.

3. Audit

Crawler, canonical, hreflang, schema, broken links: it all comes from your HTML. This course's auditor does the essentials; Core Web Vitals live in PageSpeed and CrUX, free.

It doesn't run JavaScript on purpose: it shows what a bot that doesn't render sees.

4. AI and backlinks

AI citation: the official, free option is Bing AI Performance. Paid monitors run a list of questions through the AIs; that's a sample, not an official score.

Full backlinks, with anchor and URL, only in paid tools. The Common Crawl web graph gives a rough view, by domain and months behind.

2026 yardstickA local model (Ollama) doesn't measure what ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity answer: it has neither their search nor their index.

03 · Download the 5 tools and check them

Just Python 3.10+ with the standard library. Nothing to install besides git.

Terminal · download
git clone https://github.com/inematds/inemaseo-sistemas && cd inemaseo-sistemas
python3 --version
Terminal · run the tests (no network)
for t in ferramentas/*/; do [ -d $t/tests ] && python3 -m unittest discover -s $t/tests; done
My questions × source (template)
# What I want to know about my site — <yoursite.com> — <mm/dd/yyyy>
| question | source | free? | tool / lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| how many non-branded searches find me | Search Console | yes | gsc-historico (lesson 4) |
| does Microsoft's AI cite my pages? | Bing AI Performance | yes | bing-ia (lesson 5) |
| what is broken in the HTML | the site itself | yes | auditor (lesson 2) |
| which AI bots read me | server log | yes | botlog (lesson 6) |
| how many people search <term> per month | purchased data | no | lesson 8 |

  • auditor: what is broken in the HTML your server delivers.
  • agente-ready: a 0–100 readiness score for AI, with public weights.
  • gsc-historico: non-branded searches, week by week, forever.
  • bing-ia: pages cited by Bing's AI × clicks on Google.
  • botlog: AI bots in the log, by purpose, and the fake ones.
  • Old Python: anything below 3.10 gives a syntax error. Check with python3 --version.
  • Expecting search volume: none of the five gives market volume; they measure your site.
  • Thinking you need a paid account: besides Search Console and Bing Webmaster (free), there are no accounts at all.
